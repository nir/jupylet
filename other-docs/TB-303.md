# The Roland TB-303: how it works, with sources

What we found about the **original** Roland TB-303 Bass Line (1981), gathered to build `TB303`, a faithful replica, in jupylet. It covers only the stock instrument, not its modifications (Devil Fish and others), except where a source about a modification states a stock value.

Every fact carries a tag naming its source. Anything no source gives is marked **UNSOURCED**.

## Overview

The TB-303 is a monophonic bass synthesizer with a built-in step sequencer. The signal path is short:

**sequencer → pitch CV (with slide) → VCO (saw or square) → VCF (4-pole diode ladder) → VCA → output**

Two envelope generators shape it [W]:

- **MEG**, the Main Envelope Generator, drives the filter's cutoff. On accented notes it also drives the VCA, and the *accent sweep circuit*.
- **VEG**, the Volume Envelope Generator, drives the VCA and nothing else.

Six knobs control the sound [S]: Tuning, Cut Off Freq, Resonance, Env Mod, Decay and Accent. Each note step can also carry an **accent** and a **slide**.

## Pitch: CV, tuning and slide

- The oscillator is scaled at **1 octave per volt**. The schematic's calibration notes read "TUNE: 110Hz (SET TUNING) CV OUT: 2.75V (A KEY)" and "WIDTH: 1 OCT/VOLT" [S].
- **Slide** is an RC lag on the pitch CV: "the 100K resistance of the DAC feeding a 0.22uF capacitor" [M]. That's a **22ms** time constant, and "Normally the slide time is 60 ms" [M], about 3 time constants. The lag acts on the CV in volts, which are octaves, so the pitch glides exponentially in log-frequency.
- The tuning knob's range: **UNSOURCED**.

## The oscillator (VCO)

- **Sawtooth:** the VCO core's waveform. The schematic's waveform sketch shows it between about 12V and 5.5V [S].
- **Square:** derived from the sawtooth by a single-transistor waveshaper (Q8), so it's not a true square. It's described as hollow-sounding, and its duty cycle varies with pitch. Sources: forum posts, and a textbook chapter crediting Whittle with a duty of about 45% at high notes to 71% at low notes. **Not verified** against a primary source.

## The filter (VCF)

- **Type:** a 4-pole **diode ladder**, where most synths use a transistor ladder. "In the TB-303 the lowest capacitor is half the value of the other three" [T]. The schematic agrees: C18 is 0.018µF, and C19, C24 and C26 are 0.033µF [S].
- **Slope:** 24dB/octave, but "it behaves more like an 18 dB filter over much of its audible range" [T]. That's why it's often called an 18dB filter.
- **Bass:** "The TB-303 has a weaker bass response ... In the TB-303, 32 Hz was down by 5 dB" [M]. A clone was measured with a low resonant peak around 8Hz, which [T] suggests is a large part of the 303's sound.
- **Cutoff range:** the maximum with no Env Mod is about **2.5kHz**. [M] gives 5kHz for the Devil Fish, "an octave above the typical maximum for the TB-303". Measured [O]: the Cut Off knob spans about **314Hz to 2394Hz**, exponentially.
- **The filter does not follow the note played:** "In the TB-303, the filter frequency was not affected by the pitch of the note the oscillator was playing" [M].
- **Control summing:** the control voltages are summed through resistors into an exponential converter, Q10/Q11 with Q9 [S, A]. The summing resistors [S]:
  - **R73, 100k,** with **TM3, 470k, the "FREQ" trimmer**;
  - **R63, 220k,** "driven by the wiper of the Env Mod pot" [W];
  - **R72, 100k.** The accent sweep enters through a "100k mixing resistor" [W].
- **Output:** the filter's output reaches the VCA through R122 100k with C22 0.01µF, and R121 220k with C21 0.01µF [S].
- **Resonance:** its range, and whether the stock unit self-oscillates, are **UNSOURCED**.

## The filter envelope (MEG)

- "Sharp attack and exponential decay" [W]. Its capacitor, **C62, 1µF**, charges through **R152, 100Ω**, so the attack is about **0.1ms** [S].
- **Decay:** printed on the schematic, "**DECAY VR MAX T = 2.5 sec, MIN T = 200 ms**", with a sketch showing T measured from 100% down to **10%** [S]. As time constants, T / ln 10 ≈ **87ms to 1.09s**. [M] gives "200 ms and 2 seconds", which roughly agrees.
- **On accented notes** the decay is fixed at the minimum: "On accented notes, this is shorted out and MEG runs to the relatively short time which is the same as when the Decay pot is fully anti-clockwise" [W]. The switch is in IC12, a 4066 [S, A].
- **Env Mod:** "For both normal and accented notes, the MEG affects the filter frequency via the Env Mod pot" [W]. "The circuitry does not allow this effect to be turned down to zero even when the pot is fully anti-clockwise" [W]. The measured mapping [O], with the Cut Off knob `c` and the Env Mod knob `e` from 0 to 1, in octaves:
  - `scale = (1-c)·(3.774·e + 0.737) + c·(4.195·e + 0.864)`, and
  - `cutoff = 313.8 Hz · (2394.4/313.8)^c · 2^(scale · (MEG − (0.0483·c + 0.2944)))`.

  So even at `e = 0` the envelope moves the cutoff by about 0.74–0.86 octaves, and when the MEG is low the cutoff sits slightly *below* the knob's nominal value.

## The volume envelope (VEG) and the VCA

- "Sharp attack, exponential decay, fixed decay and rather long time. Drives the VCA and does nothing else" [W]. Its decay is set by **R123, 1.5M,** and **C42, 1µF**, a **1.5s** time constant [S]. "The TB-303's VEG decay was fixed at ~ 3 to 4 seconds" [M]: 1.5s × ln 10 ≈ 3.5s to 10%, so the two agree.
- **The trigger** is smoothed by **R134, 22k,** and **C41, 0.1µF** (2.2ms) [S]. [M] measures the note as starting "between 1 and 5 ms (typically 4 ms) after the start of the gate signal", with "a 3 ms attack time".
- **The VCA** is IC15, a BA662A. The VEG enters its control input through **R131, 220k** [S].
- **The note's end:** "since the gate is off the volume drops to nothing very quickly - unless it is an accented note, in which case the Main EG drives the VCA as well" [W]. "The decay at the end of an unaccented note was about 16 ms — 8 ms of normal volume and 8 ms of linear decay" [M].

## Accent

This is the most distinctive and least understood part of the 303. On an accented note [W]:

1. **The filter envelope decays at its shortest,** as described above.
2. **The accent signal:** "the MEG voltage goes through a switch (which is only on during accented notes) and then through the Accent pot" [W]. It then does two things.
3. **Louder:** "It adds to the control current of the VCA. (Via an RC network of a 47k and a 0.033uF to soften the attack a little.) This is the primary reason why accented notes are louder" [W]. On the schematic, that path is **D27 → R120 22k → C36 0.033µF to ground → R119 47k** into the VCA's control input [S]. The VEG enters through 220k and the accent through 47k, so per volt the accent counts **220/47 ≈ 4.7 times** as much.
4. **The filter, through the accent sweep circuit:** "Via an 'Accent Sweep Circuit' it adds to the filter control current. Basically this raises the filter frequency on accented notes, to a degree controlled by the Accent pot" [W].

The accent sweep circuit [W, S]:

- The accent signal passes through the diode **D24** and **R46, 47k**, into one end of **VR4's second section, 50k**. **C13, 1µF,** hangs from the other end to ground. The pot's wiper feeds the cutoff's summing point through 100k.
- **Resonance fully anticlockwise:** the wiper is at the diode's end, and "the filter is driven largely with the output of the MEG" [W].
- **Resonance fully clockwise:** the wiper is at the capacitor, and "the pulse of voltage driving the filter is smoothed by coming from the 1uF cap as it is charged via the diode" [W], through 47k plus the 50k section [S]. The result: "The filter goes 'Wow.' It rises and falls in a quick, smooth curve" [W].
- **The diode is what makes accents build up.** The capacitor charges only through it, and drains through the pot and the 100k mixing resistor. "When you have multiple accented notes in quick succession ... Since the capacitor has not discharged fully from the one before, the second and subsequent response curves go *higher*" [W].
- Solved as a circuit with an ideal diode:
  - **Resonance fully anticlockwise:** the wiper jumps at once to about 0.41 of the accent signal.
  - **Resonance fully clockwise:** the wiper follows the capacitor, which charges toward about 0.51 of the signal with a time constant of about 49ms: 97k in parallel with the 100k load, into 1µF.

  [A] gives the same figures, 0.42 and 0.51 with 49ms.

**UNSOURCED:** how many octaves the accent sweep adds, and the true gain of the accent into the VCA. Both depend on the actual voltages of the MEG and of the accent signal, which are set by the transistor stages around Q38–Q41 and by the diodes D26–D28 [S]. We haven't solved those from the scan.

## The sequencer: gate, accent and slide timing

A 16th-note step is **6 clock pulses** [W]:

- "The gate goes high at the start of clock pulse 0 - the positive edge. The gate goes low half-way through clock pulse 3 - on its negative edge." That's 3.5 clocks on, and 2.5 off.
- "The other function of slide is that the note it is programmed on does not turn off, but the gate stays high and runs into the next."
- "The slide signal will go high 1/16 note after the start of slid note" and "will remain active until the end of the note which follows the slid note."
- **A slide doesn't retrigger the envelopes,** since the gate stays high. That follows from the gate behavior and the envelopes being gate-triggered; it's stated as a mechanism in [W], not measured.

## Conflicts between sources

| What | Values | Resolved as |
|---|---|---|
| The Resonance pot in the accent sweep | [W]'s description: a "100k pot", and "147k" when charging | **50k**: [S] marks VR4 as 50K(B) dual, and [B] and [W]'s own pot list agree. Charging goes through 97k. |
| The Decay pot | [W]'s pot list: 50K A | **1M (A)**, per [S] (VR6) and [B]. |
| The MEG's maximum decay | [M]: 2s | **2.5s to 10%**, as printed on [S]. |
| The MEG's attack | [O] uses 3ms | **About 0.1ms** (R152 × C62) [S]. [M]'s 3ms attack is the note's audible attack, which comes from the VEG and the VCA. |

## What's still unknown

- How many octaves the accent sweep adds to the cutoff, and the accent's true gain into the VCA (see *Accent*).
- The absolute voltages of the MEG and of the accent signal.
- The resonance knob's law, and whether the stock unit self-oscillates.
- The square wave's exact shape and duty cycle.
- The tuning knob's range.
- A fuller model of the filter: the diode ladder with its half-size bottom capacitor, and the coupling and high-pass stages that shape the bass.

## How `TB303` uses this

`TB303` in `jupylet/audio/synth/tb303_bassline.py` follows this document, and tags each value in its code with the same letters. The module's docstring introduces the TB-303 and the concepts its code uses. In short:

- **Envelopes:** the MEG and VEG as exponential decays that restart on each new note and run on after the gate closes. MEG time constants of 87ms–1.09s (accents at 87ms), with the Decay knob mapped logarithmically. VEG time constant of 1.5s.
- **The accent circuits:** the accent sweep circuit and the accent's VCA network, each solved component by component, with an ideal diode (`diode_rc()`).
- **Pitch and cutoff:** the slide at 22ms; Open303's measured cutoff and Env Mod mapping.
- **Knobs:** from 0 to 1, as on the instrument.
- **Main departure:** the filter is jupylet's Moog-style `LadderFilter`, not a model of the 303's diode ladder. The remaining **UNSOURCED** values are marked in the code.
